## (a) Language-Specific Challenges
The analysis reveals a significant performance gap in Arabic NER when using lightweight statistical models like xx_ent_wiki_sm. The primary challenge in Arabic is the lack of capitalization, which English models rely on heavily to identify proper nouns. In the climate dataset, names of organizations like "The Ministry of Environment" are often missed in Arabic because the model cannot distinguish them from general descriptive prose. Furthermore, Arabic’s morphological complexity causes issues; when the preposition "in" (بـ) is attached to "Jordan" (بالأردن), the spaCy model often fails to recognize the root entity, whereas the Transformer-based XLM-Roberta handles these sub-word tokens with much higher accuracy.

## (b) Implications for MENA NLP Systems
For NLP applications in Jordan and the wider MENA region, these results prove that a "one-size-fits-all" multilingual approach requires careful model selection. Relying on basic multilingual models for Arabic leads to a high "No Entities Found" rate, which could be disastrous for automated report indexing or news monitoring. Professional pipelines must prioritize Transformer-based architectures (like XLM-R or AraBERT) to ensure that Arabic entities are extracted with the same density as English ones. In a bilingual environment, engineers must implement robust preprocessing to handle Arabic diacritics and clitics to prevent the data loss observed in the spaCy outputs.
